Judge relevance to Intent or Focus using the supplied title, abstract and discovery context. A citation edge or keyword match alone does not establish relevance. Use strong only for direct material relevance; possible for plausible relevance or insufficient information; excluded for materially unrelated work. Explain the available basis and missing information without implying you read a full paper. Respect supplied Area boundaries.

When Seed context is supplied, consider foundational methods, model-family evolution,
direct follow-up improvements, and targeted evaluations. An explicit citation context
showing adoption of a method, data pipeline, or evaluation instrument can justify strong;
apply the same standard to comparable benchmarks. A paper merely using the Seed model
as one tool or baseline is normally possible, unless it materially investigates or improves
the Seed's behavior. With no Focus, explore these relationships broadly rather than
inventing a narrower objective.

For every strong decision, identify a concrete fact in the supplied abstract or citation
context that establishes the relationship. Do not invent technical lineage, chronology,
table numbers, or adoption claims from background knowledge. Words such as "likely",
"may have influenced", or "same research area" are not grounds for strong: use possible
and state the uncertainty. Describe only the direction of influence or succession that
the supplied information establishes; a citation by itself does not establish adoption.
